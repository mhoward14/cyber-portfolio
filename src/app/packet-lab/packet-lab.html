Packet Analysis Lab

A Wireshark-style workbench in the browser. Pick a threat hunt, filter the capture with display filters, inspect each packet down to the byte, and answer the questions an analyst would have to answer.

Every capture is synthetic, generated byte-for-byte in your browser from fictional hosts: internal addresses in 10.0.0.0/8, external addresses from the RFC 5737 documentation ranges, and names under the reserved .example domain. The filter bar understands a subset of Wireshark's display-filter syntax, so filters you learn here work in Wireshark too. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Filter syntax and fields

field is true when the field is present. Compare with == != > < >= <= or contains "text", and combine with &&, ||, !, and parentheses. ip.addr and tcp.port match either direction; IP fields accept CIDR blocks such as 10.20.0.0/16.

Protocols: eth, ip, tcp, udp, dns, http, tls, ftp

ip: ip.src, ip.dst, ip.addr, ip.ttl, ip.len, ip.proto

tcp: tcp.srcport, tcp.dstport, tcp.port, tcp.stream, tcp.seq, tcp.ack, tcp.len, tcp.flags, tcp.flags.syn, tcp.flags.ack, tcp.flags.fin, tcp.flags.reset, tcp.flags.push

udp: udp.srcport, udp.dstport, udp.port, udp.length, udp.stream

dns: dns.id, dns.flags.response, dns.qry.name, dns.qry.type (1 = A, 16 = TXT), dns.a, dns.txt

tls: tls.record.content_type, tls.handshake.type, tls.handshake.extensions_server_name

ftp: ftp.request, ftp.request.command, ftp.request.arg, ftp.response, ftp.response.code

frame / eth: frame.number, frame.len, frame.time_relative, frame.protocols, eth.src, eth.dst, eth.addr
