// HOW IT WORKS

Likelihood and impact each use five levels, from very low to very high. The risk rating comes from a matrix like the qualitative one in NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 (Appendix I): a risk that is very likely but would barely matter, or that would matter greatly but is very unlikely, rates lower than one that is both. Treatment follows the response options in that guide: mitigate, accept, transfer or avoid. The ratings are your judgment, so the page shows what each level means beside the choice, and the register stores only your inputs. The rating is always worked out from them.

If you have prioritized vulnerabilities, the draft button turns the P1 and P2 findings into risk statements. Exploited or public flaws on internet-facing systems become more likely, and flaws on business-critical systems become higher impact. Treat the drafts as a starting point, not a verdict. A risk register belongs to the organization, so owners and due dates are left blank in the worksheet for you to complete.
